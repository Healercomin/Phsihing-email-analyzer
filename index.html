    What you should do next

      How your score was calculated

      Every warning sign above has a fixed point value. They are added together and the total is capped at 100. Bands: 0–24 Low Risk, 25–59 Suspicious, 60–100 High Risk.

        ⚠️ Stay safe: rules for handling a suspicious email

        • Do not click links or open attachments just to see whether they are dangerous. You do not need to test them.
        • Never reply with passwords, one-time codes, bank details or personal information.
        • Verify independently. Use the organisation's official website or app — type the address yourself — or call a number you already have (from a statement, card or official site). Never use contact details from the email.
        • Report and delete. Use your email provider's “Report phishing” option, or forward the message to your IT / security team, then delete it.
        • If you already acted, change that password immediately, turn on two-factor authentication, tell your IT team, and contact your bank if money or account details were involved.

        What this tool can and cannot do

        It cannot definitively tell you whether an email is malicious or safe, and it cannot see everything: images, QR codes, hidden email internals, or where a link really leads until you visit it (which you should not do). It only examines the text and headers you paste.

        It is designed to teach you the warning signs and help you decide when to slow down and verify. A high score means “many warning signs found”, not “proven malicious”; a low score means “no common warning signs found”, not “proven safe”.
